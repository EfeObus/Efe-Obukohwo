Superstore Sales Analysis

Retail EDA & 12-Month Sales Forecasting

2014 – 2017 Data Data Analyst Portfolio Project 9,994 Order Lines

Project Overview

The Superstore dataset is a retail order-line log spanning four full years — 9,994 orders across Region, Category, Sub-Category, Segment, and Ship Mode. This project runs a full exploratory pass to find where the business actually makes and loses money, then builds a 12-month sales forecast by comparing two time-series models head-to-head on a genuine holdout rather than picking one on faith.

The Business Problem

Revenue alone doesn't tell a retailer where it's profitable. A region, state, or sub-category can look healthy on the top line while quietly losing money underneath — usually through discounting. Leadership needed to know exactly where that was happening, how much it was costing, and what the next 12 months of sales were likely to look like so inventory and staffing could be planned against a number, not a guess.

Vision

Turn 9,994 order lines into a clear map of where profit leaks out of the business, then validate two competing forecasting models on a real 6-month holdout before trusting either one with a 12-month projection.

Methodology & Technical Execution

Data Preparation

  • Date parsing: Order/Ship Date strings were day-first (D/M/YYYY) but inconsistently zero-padded — parsed explicitly with dayfirst=True rather than a fixed format string to avoid silently misreading dates.
  • Data quality: Verified no nulls, no duplicate rows, and no ship-before-order rows before analysis.

Exploratory Analysis

  • Computed profit margin by region, segment, and state; ranked all 17 sub-categories by total profit; and measured discount rate's correlation with margin at the row level.
  • Analyzed shipping delay by ship mode and region, calendar-month seasonality across all four years, order value distribution, and customer revenue concentration.

Forecasting

  • Train/holdout split: 48 months of aggregated monthly sales split into a 42-month training window and a 6-month holdout, so model comparison happens on genuinely unseen data.
  • Model comparison: Fit Prophet and auto-ARIMA (SARIMA) on the training window, scored both on MAE, RMSE, and MAPE against the holdout, and only then picked a winner.
  • Final forecast: Refit the winning model on the full 48-month series and projected 12 months forward with an 80% confidence interval.

Forecast Growth, Next 12 Months
  • Discounting, not category or region, drives losses: row-level discount rate and profit margin correlate at -0.86 — unprofitable orders carry a 48% average discount versus 8% on profitable ones. 1,871 of 9,994 order lines (18.7%) post negative profit, giving back $156,131 total.
  • Tables is the single largest profit drag: 3 of 17 sub-categories lose money overall (Tables, Bookcases, Supplies), and Tables alone gives back $17,725 — despite being a top-6 product by sales volume, meaning the demand is real and the pricing/discount structure is the problem.
  • Texas is a pricing problem, not a demand problem: it's the #3 state by sales revenue but the single biggest loss-maker overall at -$25,729.
  • Region is a margin lever, not just a volume lever: West and East convert 14-15% of sales to profit; Central converts under 8% despite not being the smallest region by revenue.
  • Prophet beat SARIMA on every metric on the 6-month holdout (20.7% MAPE vs. 27.8%) and, refit on the full series, forecasts $778,333 in sales over the next 12 months — 6.2% above the trailing 12 months, consistent with the recurring Q4 seasonal peak in the historical data.

Strategic Impact & Recommendations

Cap Discounting by Category

Furniture margin turns negative around a 20% discount and keeps falling, while Technology and Office Supplies tolerate discounting well past 30% — discount policy should be set per category, not as a single blanket ceiling.

Re-price or Re-position Tables

With real sales volume but a structural $17,725 loss, Tables needs a pricing or discount-structure fix, not a demand-generation push.

Investigate Texas Discounting Practices

Texas's loss position alongside strong revenue points to a market-specific discounting or pricing issue worth a dedicated review, separate from the national policy.

Plan Inventory and Staffing Against the Forecast

Use the Prophet 12-month projection and its recurring Q4 peak to time inventory build-up and seasonal staffing rather than reacting to the spike after it starts.
