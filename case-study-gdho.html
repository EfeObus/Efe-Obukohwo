GDHO Dataset Analysis

Humanitarian Sector Analytics & Budget-Estimator ML Pipeline

2006 – 2024 Data Data Analyst Portfolio Project 125,125 Org-Year Rows

Project Overview

The Global Database of Humanitarian Organisations (GDHO) tracks close to every organization doing humanitarian work worldwide — national NGOs, international NGOs, Red Cross/Crescent societies, and UN agencies — from 2006 through 2024. This project cleans the raw 2025 release, computes 20 statistically-backed insights about the shape and health of the sector, and builds a machine-learning pipeline that estimates an organization's budget from its operational profile, treating the dataset's own data-quality limitations as a first-class finding rather than a footnote.

The Business Problem

GDHO is one of the only datasets that shows the humanitarian sector's full long tail — not just the large organizations that publish their own financial reports. That is also its core limitation: because most tracked organizations don't publish detailed financials, GDHO itself has to estimate a great deal of what it reports. Donors, coordination bodies, and researchers need both the sector-level picture and an honest read on how much of that picture is directly reported versus modeled.

Vision

Clean and analyze 125,125 org-year rows across 7,301 organizations, surface the structural findings that matter for decision-makers, and build an independent budget-estimator model — while explicitly auditing how much of the underlying data is GDHO's own imputation rather than audited financial reporting.

Methodology & Technical Execution

Data Cleaning & Scope

  • Scope: 125,125 org-year rows × 282 columns (2006-2024), covering 7,301 unique organizations — 42 core descriptive/financial fields plus ~240 binary country-of-operation flags.
  • Provenance audit: Only ~7% of reported Operating Expenditure (OPE) values and ~5% of Staff values carry an "Actual" provenance flag — the rest are GDHO's own imputations, which directly shaped how confidently any absolute totals could be stated.

Exploratory Analysis (20 Insights)

  • Computed sector composition, budget concentration, geography, and data-quality statistics across the full panel, each backed by a computed figure rather than a visual impression.
  • Flagged structural data-quality issues head-on: a 26.5% missing "International or national" field that's cheaply inferable from Type, a % Hum field exceeding 100% for 30% of rows, and hundreds of organizations sharing an identical imputed OPE value — evidence GDHO imputes by bucket (Type/Tier/Sub-region/Year), not per organization.

Budget-Estimator ML Pipeline

  • Leakage control: Deliberately excluded every field derived from or co-dependent with the target (Tier, HumExp, % Hum, inflation-adjusted/original-currency OPE, and all *Imputed flags), since Tier alone is a near-deterministic budget band.
  • Organization-grouped evaluation: Because the same organization repeats across up to 19 years, used a GroupShuffleSplit by organization ID (80/20 orgs, zero overlap) rather than a random row split, which would leak and understate error. Model selection used 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation within the training split only.
  • Model comparison: Evaluated LightGBM, Random Forest, Ridge, and a median-dummy baseline; LightGBM won on held-out, never-seen organizations.

  • A long-tail sector: National NGOs are 81% of tracked organizations in 2024 but only 3.4% of total reported budget — median OPE spans roughly 6,780x between a national NGO (~$528K) and a UN agency (~$3.58B).
  • The sector may have peaked in 2020: Tracked organizations grew every year from 2006 (6,225) to 2020 (7,165), then fell 21% to 5,662 by 2024 — consistent with, but not conclusively caused by, the pandemic era.
  • Most reported financials are estimates: Only ~7% of OPE values carry an "Actual" flag; the rest are GDHO's own imputations, and the single most common 2024 OPE value is shared by 882 different organizations.
  • Staff is the strongest predictor of budget in the LightGBM model, ahead of Year, national/international staff split, and sub-region — consistent with the raw r=0.63 correlation found in EDA.
  • The model's accuracy comes with a caveat: because most training labels are themselves GDHO-imputed, part of the 0.796 R² reflects the model re-deriving GDHO's own imputation formula rather than discovering independently verified ground truth — documented explicitly rather than presented as a clean validation result.

Strategic Impact & Recommendations

Treat Totals as Estimates, Not Audited Figures

Any stakeholder using GDHO's absolute budget totals should present them as model-based estimates — with only ~7% of underlying figures directly reported, headline sector-spend numbers should carry that caveat visibly.

Use the Model as a Fallback, Not a Ground Truth

The LightGBM estimator is genuinely useful for organizations with no reported financials at all, but its accuracy against GDHO's own imputed labels should not be read as validation against independently audited financial reporting.

Prioritize Direct Reporting Where It's Missing

Given how much of the dataset's financial picture rests on bucket-level imputation, closing direct-reporting gaps for the largest under-reported organizations would meaningfully improve sector-wide budget accuracy.
