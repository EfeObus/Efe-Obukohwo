Building TEKVWA Money: One Codebase, Three Platforms, Real Bank Data

2026 13 min read Efe Obukohwo
React Express.js Capacitor Plaid Gemini AI

Most budgeting apps fail for a boring reason: they ask too much of the user. Connect every account, learn a categorization system, review every transaction by hand. People with a decent income still can't say where last month's money went, not because they're bad with money, but because the tool asked for more discipline than a habit-forming app should. TEKVWA Money is my attempt at the opposite bet — make tracking spending require almost no ongoing effort, and let the app do the noticing.

It's live now on web, Android, and iOS, built from a single React codebase, with a real bank-linking integration (Plaid, in Production, not a sandbox demo) and an AI layer that narrates spending patterns instead of just logging them.

💡 The Core Bet

Financial leakage — small, frequent, unnoticed expenses — is a noticing problem, not a discipline problem. So the app's job is to eliminate manual entry wherever possible (statement import, bank sync, auto-categorization that learns from corrections) and surface the pattern before the user has to go looking for it (budget-aware alerts, AI-narrated insights, weekly/monthly summaries).

One React App, Three Native Targets

The client is a single React + Vite + Zustand app. Capacitor wraps that same build into real native Android and iOS projects — not a WebView pointed at a hosted URL, but the production bundle compiled directly into the app, talking to https://tekvwamoney.com instead of a dev proxy. There's no separate mobile codebase to keep in sync, no feature drift between platforms by accident.

That constraint shaped a lot of decisions I wouldn't have had to make building a web app alone:

Biometric App Lock

Face ID / fingerprint gates the app on every open or resume, independent of the login session, with the token stored behind Android Keystore / iOS Keychain rather than a plain flag.

Home-Screen Quick Add

Long-press shortcuts deep-link straight into the Add Income/Expense form with the amount field pre-focused — Android static shortcuts and iOS UIApplicationShortcutItems, same JS handling underneath.

Offline Queuing

Adding a transaction with no connection queues it locally with a "Pending sync" badge and replays on reconnect, instead of just failing.

The two platforms don't actually behave identically, and pretending otherwise is where native wrappers usually get you in trouble. iOS's WKWebView reserves the http/https URL schemes for real remote pages, so it silently falls back to capacitor://localhost no matter what the config says — that's not a bug in this app, it's an upstream constraint, and the backend's CORS allowlist and origin-checking middleware both have to know about it explicitly rather than trusting URL.origin (which returns the literal string "null" for non-special schemes like capacitor:, per the WHATWG spec). Small platform gaps like that are exactly the kind of thing that stay invisible until a real device surfaces them.

Real Bank Data, Not a Demo

Connect Bank links a real account through Plaid, running against Plaid's Production environment on web, Android, and iOS alike — new transactions sync in about once a day, auto-categorized the same way a manually uploaded statement is, with no review step required. For people who'd rather not link an account directly, statement import covers PDF, CSV, and XLSX uploads across 24+ Canadian banks and credit unions, staging transactions for review before anything is committed, and remembering category corrections for next time.

Keeping a separate, Super-Admin-only sandbox evaluation page for poking at Plaid's test environment — completely walled off from the customer-facing flow — turned out to matter more than I expected during development. It meant I could break things against fake data as often as I wanted without ever touching a real customer's connection.

AI That Narrates, Never Calculates

AI Insights uses Gemini through Vertex AI to generate daily, weekly, and monthly reads on spending behavior. The rule I held to throughout: the model only ever narrates numbers the app has already computed deterministically in SQL — it's never asked to do arithmetic itself. Total spend, category breakdowns, budget-vs-actual, merchant frequency — all of that is calculated first, and Gemini's only job is turning verified numbers into a sentence a person will actually read. That boundary matters a lot for a finance app; a model that's occasionally wrong about a dollar figure is a much bigger problem than a model that's occasionally awkward in prose.

Alerts That Don't Cry Wolf

Budget-driven spending alerts fire at 70%, 85%, and 100%+ thresholds — falling back to an income-based comparison for anyone without a budget set. The part that took more care than the thresholds themselves: deduplication. A naive implementation re-fires the same warning every time a new expense is added past a threshold, which trains users to ignore the notification entirely. Alerts here escalate once per threshold per period instead, so a 75% warning shows up once, not on every subsequent transaction until the period resets.

What Shipping to Three App Stores Actually Costs

The unglamorous parts took the most iteration. Safe-area padding for the iPhone's Dynamic Island needed both a viewport meta tag and matching CSS on every fixed-position full-screen element individually — body padding alone doesn't reach anything with position: fixed. The biometric prompt on iOS briefly resigns and reactivates the app as part of showing its own system UI, which raced against the app's own resume-triggered lock listener and would sometimes re-lock the screen the instant it had just unlocked — fixed with an explicit "unlock in flight" guard rather than trying to out-time the race. None of this shows up in a demo; all of it shows up on a real device on day one.

iOS push notifications are the one piece still incomplete — not from a code gap, but a $99/year Apple Developer Program membership gap. The client-side registration runs on iOS today and is harmless; it just can't be delivered to yet. Everything else — biometric lock, Quick Add shortcuts, Plaid, AI Insights — is verified live on both platforms.
